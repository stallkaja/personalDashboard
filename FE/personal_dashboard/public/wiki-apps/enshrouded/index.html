What 1.0 changed

  • Every skill tree was reset. The tree is now one map: a Core in the middle with Warrior, Ranger and Mage areas around it.
  • New skills: Aerial Wand Attack, Wand Evasion Attack, Blast and Cast, Advanced Necromancy, Totem Mastery, Puncture, Barrage, Survival Instinct, Airborne Architect.
  • New spells: Mana Drain, Entangle, Pillar of Flames, Blizzard, Ball Lightning, three elemental totems, Summon Hollow Skeleton. Tier 7 Eternal spells and Eternal arrows need no ammo.
  • New endgame region, Cinder's Cradle, with 12 Shroud Roots, a new enemy faction and a new survivor. Two new Hollow Halls in Albaneve and Veilwater.
  • Upgradeable chests, renamable markers, free-flying build mode, and the Sparkworks logic system for doors and traps.

Picking a style as a solo player

  • Safest solo picks: Sword & Shield Tank and Ranger (bow). Both have their own sustain and can stay out of trouble.
  • Strong but slow start: Wand/Staff Mage feels weak until about level 15, then becomes the top damage option.
  • Hard solo: pure Healer (little damage) and pure Assassin (backstabs need someone else holding aggro). Run them as hybrids; each card says how.
  • Everyone: carry a melee weapon, a bow, and a wand. Swapping is free, and some enemies and puzzles need range.
  • Respec is cheap at the Flame Altar, so try things. Use the Skill Builder to plan first.

Base basics that pay off every session

  • Rested buff raises max stamina and stamina regen. Its length comes from warmth (fire), shelter (walls and roof), and comfort (bed, chairs, tables, rugs, decor). Wait by the fire before you leave.
  • Food: eat three foods of different types. Only one food per type (meat, fruit, liquid, herb, sweet) can be active. Dessert Stomach adds a fourth slot.
  • Every survivor needs a bed inside your altar's area. Summon them with the Summoning Staff.
  • Altars are your fast travel. Each Flame level allows more altars (4 at Flame 2, up to 16 at Flame 11). Drop a small outpost altar in each new region.
  • New in 1.0: storage chests upgrade in place, chests and map markers can be renamed, free-flying build mode (Airborne Architect), and the Sparkworks logic system.

Where to put your main base

  • Your first altar goes where the story puts it (the Plains). That spot is fine for levels 1 to 10.
  • For a long-term home, pick flat ground near the Springlands and Revelwood border: close to water, copper, clay and a fast-travel point, with a cliff or tower nearby for glider launches.
  • Keep crafting stations together so one storage wall feeds all of them.
  • Upgrading an altar widens its building area. Do that on the main base before you build big.

    How this planner works

    • The tree matches the in-game 1.0 tree: node positions, connections, costs and ranks come from the game files (read from PinDrop's 1.0 planner, Oct 28, 2026). Icons are simple stand-ins, not the game's art.
    • As in game, every skill must connect back to one of the 12 purple starting points. A node's ring lights up when you can buy it. Click a node for details; double-click buys a rank. "Buy path to here" buys the cheapest route to a skill that's far away.
    • You can't refund a skill that others branch from. Refund the outer ones first.
    • The Build helper (on the map) is a chat with Claude that edits this tree for you: "start from the Two-Hander at level 20", "make it tankier", "swap greatsword skills for hammer ones". It sees every skill's cost and your points, explains each change, and Undo reverts its last edit. It runs through the dashboard's AI connection (Claude when an API key is set, otherwise the local Ollama model).
    • Points: 2 per level from level 2, 1 per Shroud Root (60) and 3 per Elixir Well (18), for 212 at level 50. The Root and Well estimate assumes you clear each region at its level; untick it to enter your real count.
    • Recommended paths include the connecting nodes they need, so their costs match the game. Effect text for most 1.0 skills isn't published yet; text marked Pre-1.0 data describes the skill before 1.0.
